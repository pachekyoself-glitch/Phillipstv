# Accredited-source approval policy

**Release status: BLOCKED — no educational item is approved or bundled.** This project is an implementation plan, not an accredited curriculum, a certified child-safe product, or an endorsed Harvard/FSU app.

The owner requires material approved by an accredited source. The policy therefore requires **documented approval of each exact item and intended use by an accredited educational institution/source**, not merely a citation or a model's judgment. University accreditation applies to the institution; it does not approve our TV adaptation. Parent review is a separate requirement and cannot replace the educational approval.

## What must be recorded locally

1. Exact title, source publisher and original URL; immutable item/version and SHA-256 of every local file.
2. Institution, accreditor, official accreditation evidence URL, and verification date. Confirm current status before each new content release.
3. Approval evidence for the exact item, age group, learning objective, presentation format and intended offline use. Preserve source-published educational review where it explicitly covers this use, or written confirmation from an authorized source representative. Do not infer approval from an institutional domain, logo or accreditation listing.
4. Reproduction, adaptation, offline storage, display and redistribution rights. An educationally approved item can still be prohibited from being copied, altered or published. Preserve the applicable license or permission evidence.
5. Parent review of the entire item, imagery/audio, language, reading level, objective, age mode, interaction and accessibility, followed by a separate explicit approval.
6. Technical validation: local allowlisted media type, safe decoding, no executable/HTML/web content, size/duration limits, no links, no feeds, no ads or telemetry.

A publisher's public classroom activity is a **candidate**, not automatically an approved TV lesson. Adaptation, narration, translation, generated quiz questions, image changes and excerpts create a new version requiring renewed review and rights. The developer must not invent approval evidence or claim the source endorsed the product.

## Current candidates and limitations

- Harvard Center on the Developing Child publishes an [activity guide with 3–5 and 5–7 age bands](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/handouts-tools/activities-guide-enhancing-and-practicing-executive-function-skills/). Harvard is listed by [NECHE](https://www.neche.org/institutions/harvard-university/). This supports provenance for executive-function activities; it does not approve TV lessons or cover the entire requested curriculum.
- Florida Center for Reading Research publishes [Pre-K through grade-five activity resources](https://fcrr.org/fcrr-student-center-activities). [FSU's official accreditation statement](https://www.fsu.edu/about/accreditation) identifies SACSCOC accreditation and a prospective HLC process; do not treat an application as accreditation already awarded. FCRR imposes restrictions on alteration and reposting. No files are copied into this repository or adapted for TV.

These primary pages were checked 2026-11-04. Additional mathematics, science, nature and geography sources are **UNSOURCED** under this strict policy. Government or museum resources can be authoritative but do not automatically meet the owner's accredited-source requirement; obtain qualifying approval or an explicit revised owner criterion before adding them.

## Approval workflow

Research on the parent PC → collect exact-item institutional approval and rights → quarantine a local copy → validate bytes/type → parent preview in adult context → record independent educational/rights/parent approvals → sign a manifest with a parent-controlled key → import only the reviewed bundle → verify signature/hash again → activate only for the approved age mode.

Rejected, expired, modified, unsigned, unreviewed or ambiguous items never appear in child search or browse results. Empty approved library shows a neutral offline message and requires parent setup. No automatic online fallback, recommendations, generated answers or downloads.

Approval evidence and personal reviewer information stay local; public code contains only blank schemas and source metadata. Obtain actual institutional review through an authorized human process. This task does not authorize sending email or representing the family to an institution.
